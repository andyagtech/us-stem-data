10 stages · 75 external datasets

Permanent residence
Both halves now. 74.6% of employment-based green cards go to people already in the country, against 5.4% on the family route. And the queue is measured: India's EB-2 wait is 12.7 years against 1.2 for everyone else, from no wait at all before 2005. lpr_adjustments · visa_bulletin · perm_pipeline

10
Retention and outcomes
Of those on a temporary visa at their doctorate, 58.3% now hold citizenship or a green card and only 9.8% remain on one. Within a cohort pay is level; reported job insecurity is not. And the workforce they join is finally measured too: foreign-born workers are 19.2% of all employment and 29.0% of computer and mathematical occupations. doctorate_outcomes · working_conditions · nativity_labour

Nine of ten stages answerable, and the gap that made the argument one-sided is closed: the domestic workforce is measured, so the foreign figures can be read against something. Only the upstream stage is untouched, and by choice.

Five claims that changed under checking

Each of these was built, believed, and then corrected. They are listed because the correction is the part worth trusting.

Claim as first builtWhat checking showedWhere it stands
Foreign doctorate holders are paid less
A $30,000 pooled gap.		 Visa holders skew recent, and splitting on current citizenship counts everyone who naturalised as a citizen. Within a graduation cohort the gap is near zero — $103,000 against $100,000 for the newest. Withdrawn. The cost difference is the payroll-tax exemption, not the wage.
Visa holders work longer hours
Tested against the SDR.		 Long hours are less common among them. But the public variable is topcoded at "more than 40", so a 41-hour week and an 80-hour week are one value — and the claim is about the distance between them. Restated: the measure cannot settle the question, in either direction.
Most states cut higher-education funding
Only 13 of 50 did.		 The panel opens in 2010, a post-crash trough. The median state fell 11.1% between 2008 and 2010 and was still 5.7% below 2008 in 2023 — measured from 2008 the sign reverses. Window declared. The premise fails before the correlation does.
FY2022 visa issuance doubled
822,262 F-1, 412,004 H-1B.		 The later State Department sheets carry "Totals for Asia" subtotals and a grand total that an exact-match filter missed, so every country was counted twice. True figures 411,131 and 206,002. The sheet's own total is now a checksum on every year.
Michigan is 90.3% foreign at doctorate
From the funding join.		 Fourteen survey names cover several campuses. Keying on the name collapsed Ann Arbor, Dearborn and Flint into one record and reported whichever came last. Fixed via institution ID to campus name. Michigan reads 41.2%.
Four more, caught before they shipped. The graduate-student survey uses the token forgn in two sheets with two different meanings — the student's citizenship in one, a foreign source of funds in the other — which read wrongly would have manufactured the federal-money-to-foreign-nationals crossing this project correctly reports as unavailable. And the CPS occupation table puts native-born in the fourth column, not the second, with every row a percent of its own group rather than a headcount: taken literally, computer occupations read 61.4% foreign-born against a true 29.0%. Joining the two research surveys onto institutions was first prototyped with subset matching on normalised names, which resolves 90% of the dollars and puts Florida A&M’s money in the University of Florida’s row, Kutztown’s in the University of Pennsylvania’s and the State University of New York Maritime College’s in New York University’s; it was thrown out for exact matching plus a hand-checked list, at the cost of leaving 1.7% and 3.1% of dollars unresolved and saying so. And counting doctorates across all fields silently doubled every total, because IPEDS files a CIP 99 grand-total row repeating each detail row — the core-STEM filter had been excluding it by accident rather than by intent.

Tier 3 · Not collectable — the asks on the Data Needs page

  • Cap status per employer. Recorded on the I-129, published only as national category totals. Two independent proxies disagree in both directions.
  • Graduate applications and admissions. No federal collection exists, which is why falling domestic conversion cannot be attributed to choice, rejection or funding.
  • Citizens separated from permanent residents at conferral. Collected in three statuses, published in two. Available after graduation, never at the degree.
  • Federal funding crossed with citizenship. Confirmed absent by reading the survey's own column list, not assumed.
  • L-1 and O-1 petitions by employer. Neither requires a labour condition application and USCIS publishes an employer hub for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B but not these. The ask is specific: the petitioner field already on Form I-129, from the system that produces the H-1B hub. No new collection.
  • Country of birth for any nonimmigrant class. Every visa series is by citizenship, so a naturalised citizen cannot be distinguished from a native-born one anywhere in the data.

The limits that govern everything

These recur across the analysis and are stated wherever the figures appear. Each one bounds a conclusion the data would otherwise seem to support.

Institutions, not students

Research expenditure is recorded at the institution and never at a person. The sentence is "federal money is at these institutions", never "paid these students".

Found, not committed

Enforcement counts violations discovered, which needs a complaint. A falling line is equally consistent with fewer complaints from workers who depend on the employer they would report.

A visa is not a person

Issuances include re-stamping for travel, degrees are not people, and an adjustment of status does not name the visa held before it.

Proxy, not measurement

Research assistantships stand in for federal funding because no published field crosses agency with citizenship. Strong proxy, stated as one.

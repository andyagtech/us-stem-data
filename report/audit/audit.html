Coverage, stage by stage

10 stages · 41 external datasets

The stages are a real sequence — a person moves through them in order — so the numbering carries information rather than decoration. Coverage is judged by whether the stage can be answered from data in hand, not by how many files sit under it.

K–12 and undergraduate entry
Still nothing, and still by choice. The project begins at the bachelor's degree, so whether domestic supply is constrained upstream — by who enters STEM, or by school mathematics — remains invisible here.

Degrees conferred
Now thirty years at both graduate levels, not just the doctorate. The master's non-resident share went 24.4% to 45.9% between 1995 and 2025 against 32.7% to 37.5% for the doctorate, which peaked in 2021 and has fallen since. Permanent residence
Both halves now. 74.6% of employment-based green cards go to people already in the country, against 5.4% on the family route. And the queue is measured: India's EB-2 wait is 12.7 years against 1.2 for everyone else, from no wait at all before 2005. lpr_adjustments · visa_bulletin · perm_pipeline

Retention and outcomes
Of those on a temporary visa at their doctorate, 58.3% now hold citizenship or a green card and only 9.8% remain on a temporary visa. Within a cohort, pay is level; reported job insecurity is not. doctorate_outcomes · working_conditions · stay_rates

Three claims that changed under checking

Each of these was built, believed, and then corrected. They are listed because the correction is the part worth trusting.

Claim as first builtWhat checking showedWhere it stands
Foreign doctorate holders are paid less
A $30,000 pooled gap.		 Visa holders skew recent, and splitting on current citizenship counts everyone who naturalised as a citizen. Within a graduation cohort the gap is near zero — $103,000 against $100,000 for the newest. Withdrawn. The cost difference is the payroll-tax exemption, not the wage.
Visa holders work longer hours
Tested against the SDR.		 Long hours are less common among them. But the public variable is topcoded at "more than 40", so a 41-hour week and an 80-hour week are one value — and the claim is about the distance between them. Restated: the measure cannot settle the question, in either direction.
Michigan is 90.3% foreign at doctorate
From the funding join.		 Fourteen survey names cover several campuses. Keying on the name collapsed Ann Arbor, Dearborn and Flint into one record and reported whichever came last. Fixed via institution ID to campus name. Michigan reads 41.2%.
A fourth, caught before it shipped. The graduate-student survey uses the token forgn in two sheets with two different meanings: the student's citizenship in one, a foreign source of funds in the other. Read wrongly it would have manufactured the federal-money-to-foreign-nationals crossing that this project correctly reports as unavailable.

Tier 2 · Buildable, and would change what the work can say

  • An executive summary. Thirty-two pages is long for a congressional staffer. The material now supports a two-page front: the master's shift, the payroll-tax gap, the 12.7-year queue, and the $19.4bn.
  • Put the new datasets in the assistant's bundle. It can discuss the enforcement and queue findings from its knowledge files but cannot query them — no chart, no filter by state or employer.
  • Restore the LCA archive and match the 2,403 sponsor names against it, for a third independent read on cap-exempt filing from the DOL side.
  • Wage levels on the LCA record. The enforcement data shows violations found; the LCA prevailing-wage level shows what was promised. Crossing them would test whether low-level wage claims cluster where violations are later found.

Tier 3 · Not collectable — the asks on the Data Needs page

  • Cap status per employer. Recorded on the I-129, published only as national category totals. Two independent proxies disagree in both directions.
  • Graduate applications and admissions. No federal collection exists, which is why falling domestic conversion cannot be attributed to choice, rejection or funding.
  • Citizens separated from permanent residents at conferral. Collected in three statuses, published in two. Available after graduation, never at the degree.
  • Federal funding crossed with citizenship. Confirmed absent by reading the survey's own column list, not assumed.

The limits that govern everything

These recur across the analysis and are stated wherever the figures appear. Each one bounds a conclusion the data would otherwise seem to support.

Institutions, not students

Research expenditure is recorded at the institution and never at a person. The sentence is "federal money is at these institutions", never "paid these students".

Found, not committed

Enforcement counts violations discovered, which needs a complaint. A falling line is equally consistent with fewer complaints from workers who depend on the employer they would report.

A visa is not a person

Issuances include re-stamping for travel, degrees are not people, and an adjustment of status does not name the visa held before it.

Proxy, not measurement

Research assistantships stand in for federal funding because no published field crosses agency with citizenship. Strong proxy, stated as one.

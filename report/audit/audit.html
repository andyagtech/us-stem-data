One finding reversed

The SDR was parsed to complete retention, and the first cut of it produced a headline that did not survive its own control.

Doctorate cohortNative-citizen median Visa-at-degree medianGap
2015 or later$100,000$103,000−$3,000
2010–14$120,000$118,000+$2,000
2005–09$125,000$125,000$0
2000–04$139,000$126,000+$13,000
The pooled gap was $30,000; within cohort it is near zero. Two errors had to be removed to see it. Splitting on current citizenship counts everyone who naturalised as a citizen, leaving the non-citizen group in older cohorts a residue of those who never did — that produced gaps of $80,000 describing selection, not pay. And visa holders skew recent, so an uncontrolled comparison is mostly comparing career stages. At the doctorate level, “foreign graduates are cheaper labour” is not supported. The OPT and cap-exempt findings are a different population and stand unchanged.

Tier 2 · Buildable, and would change what the assistant can do

  • Put the new datasets in the Lambda bundle. The assistant can now discuss the concurrent-petition finding from its knowledge base but cannot query it — no chart from the sponsor ranking, no filter by state. It ships eight files and fetches nothing at runtime.
  • Restore the LCA archive and match the 2,403 sponsor names against it. A third independent read on cap-exempt filing, from the DOL side rather than USCIS. Needs a 4 GB restore from S3.
  • An executive summary for the briefing. Twenty-one pages is long for a congressional staffer; the material supports two pages leading with the doctorate leak and the 33× concurrent finding, with the rest as appendix.

Tier 3 · Not collectable — the asks on the Data Needs page

  • Cap status per employer. USCIS records it on the I-129 and publishes only national category totals. Two independent proxies disagree in both directions — 130 employers against 3,075 — which is the evidence that no outside party can derive it.
  • Graduate applications and admissions. No federal collection exists. This is why the falling doctorate conversion cannot be attributed to choice, rejection or funding.
  • Citizens separated from permanent residents. The SED collects three statuses; every published table collapses the first two. IPEDS does not collect it at all.
  • Assistantship funding linked to citizenship. Narrowed, not closed. GSS now tells us the agency and the mechanism — federal money is research assistantships, NIH and NSF are 62% of it. What no published field says is which agency funded a foreign national.
  • OPT by institution as a census. The public series is a Top-100 ranking, so school totals are a floor rather than a total.
  • O-1 and L-1 by employer. USCIS publishes employer hubs for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B, and none for O-1.

The three limits that govern everything

These recur across the analysis and are stated wherever the figures appear. They are not caveats added for safety; each one bounds a conclusion the data would otherwise seem to support.

Cap status is unpublished

Every public cap-exempt employer list, including this project's, infers status from employer type. The commercial services selling it are inferring too.

IPEDS counts degrees, not people

Progression figures are ratios between two populations, never a tracked cohort. The SDR is the one exception, and it begins at the doctorate — nothing follows a person into one.

No admissions data exists

Whether domestic applicants are declining, being declined, or not applying is unanswerable from published federal data — and that is the question underneath the argument.

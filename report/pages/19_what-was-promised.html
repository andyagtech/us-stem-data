10. What was promised

Two in three H-1B filings name the bottom of the legal wage range

Every H-1B filing states a prevailing-wage level for the occupation, from I to IV. Level I is the entry rate, Level IV the experienced one. Across 1,150,107 certified filings in 2019 and 2023, 64.6% named Level I or Level II.

Certified H-1B filings by prevailing-wage level. Source: DOL Office of Foreign Labor Certification disclosure data.

Among the largest filers the concentration is higher still: Tata Consultancy files 86.2% of its certified applications at Level I or II and Wipro 90.0%, across 2,973 employers filing at least 50 times.

What those levels pay, against the occupation

The levels are positions in a distribution. Setting the wage promised at each level beside what the occupation actually pays — the national median for everyone doing that job — gives the size of the difference. 73.3% of the 730,738 filings that could be priced promise less than the median wage for their own occupation.

Filing at Level I or II is entirely lawful. It is the cheapest legal way to fill a role, and this page reports what employers promised, not what they paid. Of the filings, 64% carried both a usable occupation code and wage and could be priced. The obvious next question — whether low wage levels predict later violations — cannot be answered here: only six of these employers appear in the published enforcement extract, which carries just its largest cases, and enforcement is not random to begin with, since large employers and staffing firms are investigated more and both skew toward low levels. Six matches cannot support a correlation, and none is claimed.

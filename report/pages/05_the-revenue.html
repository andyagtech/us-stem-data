4. The revenue

The master’s is the level that pays. Almost none of it is funded, and the billings have quadrupled

Page {{page:balance}} shows where the balance moved. This is why the level it moved at is the master’s. A doctoral student is normally paid to be there — page {{page:whopays}}. A master’s student normally is not: the Survey of Graduate Students puts master’s assistantship rates at 7.0% on a research assistantship and 6.9% on a teaching one, so roughly 86% hold neither and pay their own way.

This is sticker price, not receipts: one year of published out-of-state graduate tuition multiplied by the degrees conferred, not net of discounts, waivers or aid, and not what any institution banked. A master’s normally runs 1.5 to 2 years, so the figure for a whole programme is larger than the annual one shown. What the series establishes is the direction and the scale of the difference between the two levels, not an institution’s revenue.

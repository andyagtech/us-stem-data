13. Data needs

Seven things that would settle questions this document cannot

Each item was searched for before being listed. In every case the agency collects the information; none of it is published in a usable form.

1 · Cap status on the petition record — USCIS

Form I-129 records whether a petition is cap-exempt and on what ground. The Report to Congress publishes national totals by category; the Employer Data Hub names every employer but carries no cap field. So the 24,073 affiliated-nonprofit petitions on page {{page:exemption}} cannot be attributed to any organisation. Would settle: which employers use the exemption, at what scale, and whether use is concentrated.

2 · Federal funding crossed with citizenship — NSF

The mechanism half is answered: federal money reaches doctoral students overwhelmingly through research assistantships, 52,428 against 1,041 teaching ones, which is what licenses pages {{page:whopays}} and {{page:supporttrend}}. The crossing is not. GSS reports support by agency and enrolment by citizenship, never together; the SED records the assistantship but not which award paid. No published field says which agency funded a foreign national. Would settle: what federal research funding spends per doctoral student, by citizenship — turning a seven-year proxy into a measurement.

3 · Citizens separated from permanent residents at conferral

Available after graduation — the Survey of Doctorate Recipients records four categories, native citizen, naturalised, permanent resident, temporary visa — but not at conferral. Every published SED table collapses the first two, and IPEDS does not collect the distinction at all: a permanent resident is counted under their race. “Domestic” in the degree counts throughout this document therefore means citizens and permanent residents together. Would settle: the citizen share of U.S. graduate STEM degrees, which no published source reports.

4 · OPT by institution, for every institution — DHS

SEVP publishes a Top 100 ranking. An institution outside it is absent rather than zero, so school totals are a floor and movement between years may only reflect the cutoff. Complete school-level data exists but is released to school officials for their own institution, or by FOIA. Would settle: OPT and STEM OPT participation for every institution.

5 · Graduate applications and admissions — NCES

IPEDS counts degrees conferred. Its Admissions component covers first-time undergraduates only, and no federal collection records who applied to graduate programmes, who was admitted, or who was offered funding. Would settle: whether domestic applicants are declining, being declined, or not applying — the question behind this document, and the one it cannot answer.

6 · L-1 and O-1 petitions by employer — USCIS

For H-1B this document can name the employer three times over: what it requested, what USCIS granted, what the Labor Department found. For L-1 and O-1 it cannot name the employer once. Neither classification requires a labour condition application, so the Labor Department never sees them, and USCIS publishes an employer data hub for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B but not for these. The closest available substitute is the destination state declared on arrival — L-1 admissions concentrate in Texas, California, New York and Michigan — which is a location, not an employer. Would settle: which companies transfer the most staff into the United States without a wage test, and whether O-1 growth sits in the industries most constrained by the H-1B cap.

7 · Student visas by institution — State and DHS

The State Department reports visa issuance by consulate and nationality and never by school; SEVP publishes school-level figures for OPT alone. So the institutions that bring in the most foreign students cannot be identified directly. What can be shown is who enrols them, from IPEDS, and who places the most into STEM OPT afterwards — two different populations, neither of which is an F-1 count. Would settle: where the student pipeline actually enters, which is the first stage of everything else in this document.

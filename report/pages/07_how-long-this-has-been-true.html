5. How long this has been true

This is not a snapshot. The federally funded route through a doctorate has tilted toward visa holders in every year on record

The gap on page {{page:whopays}} is a single year. The Survey of Earned Doctorates asks the same question with every release, so the series below is built from seven of them, 2018 through 2024: the pattern holds in all seven, and the visa share rises across them.

Visa holders are now 47.2% of everyone funded principally by a research assistantship, against 46.4% in 2018 — approaching half of the federally funded doctoral workforce.

The SED records that a student was funded by a research assistantship, not which award paid. The federal link comes from the Survey of Graduate Students and Postdoctorates: 52,428 federally supported research assistantships against 1,041 federally supported teaching assistantships. The series runs on all fields because NCSES revised the field taxonomy in 2023, which would split a science-and-engineering series across two definitions; on the S&E rollup the 2024 gap is wider still, 35.4% against 53.6%.

4. From non-immigrant to immigrant

The student visa is not a temporary category. For most who finish a doctorate on one, it is the first step to permanent residence

Every count on page {{page:balance}} is a visa count, which invites the assumption that these are visitors. The Survey of Doctorate Recipients follows the same people afterwards. Of those who earned a U.S. doctorate while on a temporary visa, 58.3% now hold citizenship or permanent residence — and only 9.8% are still on a temporary visa.

Read the two pages together. A non-resident share of 44.8% of graduate STEM degrees does not measure temporary visitors passing through. It measures the entry point of a route to settlement, and 86% of those who stay complete it.

The survey covers every cohort from the 1970s on, so the balance between staying and leaving describes the whole period rather than any single year. Recent graduates have had less time to naturalise, which if anything understates the eventual rate. Status is recorded at the doctorate and again at survey; nothing here identifies which visa or which route was used, and the SED does not follow master’s recipients at all — so the transition is measured at doctorate level and applied to the larger population only by inference.

6. Around the cap

The capped route has stopped growing; the uncapped one has not

The H-1B is limited by statute to 85,000 new petitions a year. The O-1, for workers of “extraordinary ability”, has no numerical limit at all. Over the five years where both agencies count the same way, approvals moved in opposite directions: H-1B petitions -1%, O-1A petitions +42%.

Petitions approved, indexed to 2019 = 100. H-1B 389,378 to 386,340; O-1A 6,670 to 9,490. Indexed because the two differ by a factor of forty. O-1A is the sciences, education, business and athletics category, excluding the arts. Source: USCIS.

O-1 visas issued, 2005–2024

O-1 visas issued at U.S. consulates (O-1A and O-1B combined). 6,712 in 2005 to 19,457 in 2024, an increase of 190%. Source: U.S. State Department, Nonimmigrant Visas Issued by Classification.

The intracompany-transfer route moved the other way: L-1 visas issued fell from 84,532 in 2007 to 71,799 in 2024, -15%. The growth is specific to the uncapped extraordinary-ability category, not to employment visas generally.

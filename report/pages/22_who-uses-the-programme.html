11. Who uses the programme

The largest H-1B users request the bottom of the wage range, and are refused more often

Three agencies hold three pieces of this and never publish them together. The Labor Department certifies what an employer asked for and at which wage level; USCIS approves or denies the petition; the Wage and Hour Division records what it found on inspection. Joined by employer:

The split runs along one line. The outsourcing and staffing firms file most of their applications at the bottom two wage levels and are refused at seven to eighteen percent; the product companies file lower shares at the bottom and are refused at one or two.

Totals are floors. The three agencies share no identifier and each company files under several spellings — one appears eighteen times in the USCIS file alone — so names are normalised for case, punctuation and legal form and matched exactly, with nothing fuzzy-matched. Where a firm is missing from one source that means it is absent from that published extract, not that its count is zero. And no equivalent data exists for L-1 or O-1: neither requires a labour condition application and USCIS publishes no employer hub for either — page {{page:dataneeds2}}.

What follows from it, and what the data will not support

The mechanism, in one line

Graduate study is the entry point; the work visa is cheaper to the employer than an American at the same salary; and the green card that ends it is filed by that employer, for a named job. An Indian national reaching the front of the EB-2 queue has waited 12.7 years — a category that was current before 2005 — against 1.2 for everyone else. Through all of it, leaving the employer means starting again. State Department Visa Bulletin. Page {{page:leverage}}.

Where enforcement is

The Labor Department has found H-1B violations in 2,887 investigations since 2000, agreeing $123m in back wages for 13,640 employees. Measured against the size of the programme, it looks less often than it used to: 2.4 investigations per 10,000 H-1B visas issued in 2024, against 26.2 at the peak. DOL WHISARD. Page {{page:enforcement}}.

What this document does not claim

  • Not that foreign graduates are paid less. Compared within a graduation cohort, pay is level. That comparison was run, and the first version of it — a $30,000 gap — was withdrawn once cohort was controlled.
  • Not that federal grants paid named foreign students. Research expenditure is recorded at the institution and never at a person. The claim is about institutions and their graduate operations in bulk.
  • Not that violations rose or fell. Enforcement counts what was found, which needs a complaint. A falling line is equally consistent with fewer complaints from workers who depend on the employer they would report.
  • Not that Americans left STEM. Domestic output rose at every level measured. What fell is the domestic share, and the rate at which American bachelor’s graduates go on to a doctorate.

The three things no federal source will answer

Cap-exempt status per employer, which USCIS records and does not publish. Graduate applications and admissions, which no agency collects — so falling domestic conversion cannot be attributed to choice, rejection or funding. And citizens separated from permanent residents at conferral, collected in three categories and published in two. Each is set out on page {{page:dataneeds}}.

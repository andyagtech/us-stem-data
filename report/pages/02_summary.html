Summary · one

Five findings, each from a federal source

1 · The shift is at master’s level, and it has not turned

Non-residents took 24.4% of core-STEM master’s degrees in 1995 and 45.9% in 2025. The doctorate moved +4.8 points over the same thirty years, peaked in 2021 and has fallen since. American output rose at both levels — master’s +189% — so this is a change in share, not an American retreat. IPEDS Completions. Page {{page:balance}}.

2 · The federally funded route is the one Americans take least

Research assistantships are how federal money reaches doctoral students: 52,428 federally supported research assistantships against 1,041 teaching ones. They fund 48.9% of visa holders against 29.3% of citizens — a gap of 19.6 points that has not fallen below 19.3 in any of the seven years on record. NSF SED and GSS. Pages {{page:whopays}}, {{page:supporttrend}}.

3 · The money and the enrolment are in the same institutions

At 108 institutions a majority of graduate students hold a temporary visa. Those institutions hold $19.4bn of federal research expenditure — 32.9% of the matched total — and 210,870 graduate students. A second model sits beside it: at the highest-share master’s programmes there is almost no federal research money and $3.36bn a year in published tuition billed to non-resident master’s graduates. NSF HERD and GSS; IPEDS. Pages {{page:funding}}, {{page:tuitionmodel}}.

4 · An American graduate is the more expensive hire at the same salary

Wages earned on Optional Practical Training carry no Social Security or Medicare tax. The employer saves 7.65% of payroll — $7,650 per $100,000 — across 289,938 authorisations in 2024. Within a graduation cohort the two groups earn about the same, so the difference is the tax treatment, not the wage. IRC 3121(b)(19); DHS SEVP; NSF SDR. Page {{page:fica}}.

5 · The student visa is the front of a route to permanent residence

58.3% of those who earned a U.S. doctorate on a temporary visa now hold citizenship or a green card; only 9.8% remain on a temporary visa. Nationally, 74.6% of employment-based green cards go to people already in the country, against 5.4% on the family route. NSF SDR; DHS Yearbook. Pages {{page:transition}}, {{page:adjust}}.

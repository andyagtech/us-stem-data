7. Where the money is

The federal research money and the foreign enrolment are in the same institutions

Page {{page:model}} shows where the visa share is highest. This asks a different question: whether those are also the institutions the taxpayer funds. Sorting every matched institution into quartiles by federal research and development expenditure gives a clear answer at doctoral level.

477 institutions matched between the enrolment survey and the R&D survey. Shares are pooled within each quartile, so a large programme counts for more than a small one. Sources: NSF GSS 2024; NSF HERD FY2024 table 14.

The best-funded quartile enrols 167,396 doctoral students against 7,631 in the least-funded, and a 43.6% visa share against 20.5%. The public money and the foreign enrolment are not in different places.

The very highest master’s shares belong to institutions with almost no federal research money — two of the highest of all, Harrisburg University and Central Missouri, do not appear in the R&D survey at all. Those programmes are funded by tuition, not by grants, which is a different mechanism from the one in the table above; both are real and they should not be merged. HERD reports R&D expenditure, not all federal money and not student support. 172 institutions could not be matched by name, and unmatched does not mean unfunded.

7. Who holds the leverage

The employer holds the visa. Losing the job means losing the right to remain in the country, and the worker knows it

The payroll-tax gap on page {{page:fica}} is a price difference. This is a bargaining difference, and it is larger. H-1B status is tied to one employer: the employer files the petition, and on termination the worker has 60 days to find another sponsor or leave the country (8 CFR 214.1(l)(2)). Permanent residence is filed by the employer too, for a named job. An American in the next chair can resign on two weeks’ notice and lose nothing but the job.

The queue is what turns a rule into a decade. India’s EB-2 category was current — no wait at all — through 2004. It passed three years by 2007, five by 2013 and ten by 2016, and stood at 12.7 years in 2023. Every year of that is a year in which leaving the employer means starting again.

Dissatisfaction, doctorate holders working in the U.S.

Share reporting dissatisfaction, by citizenship status now. Workers on a temporary visa are 1.6 times as likely to be dissatisfied with their job security as citizens doing comparable work, and markedly more dissatisfied with pay. 63,090 employed respondents. Source: NSF Survey of Doctorate Recipients.

Two boundaries. This measures the structure of dependence and what workers report about it; no dataset records whether a visa holder accepted worse terms or declined to complain, and none is claimed here. And these respondents are doctorate holders, many of them academic postdocs — not the larger H-1B population, most of whom hold a bachelor’s or master’s and are not surveyed this way. On hours, this survey cannot settle the question either way: its top category is simply “more than 40”, so a forty-one-hour week and an eighty-hour week are the same answer, and both figures are self-reported to a federal survey by people whose right to remain depends on an employer.

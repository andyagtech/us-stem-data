2. The domestic pipeline

Americans are not leaving STEM. They are stopping before the doctorate

Domestic core-STEM completions rose at every level between 2011 and 2025. The shortfall is not in how many Americans earn STEM degrees.

What changed is where they stop. Of a domestic bachelor's cohort, the share going on to a master's rose from 23.88% to 26.63%. The share going on to a research doctorate fell from 5.41% to 4.7% — a decline of about an eighth.

Fields differ, and two here run against the pattern: mechanical engineering's domestic output grew faster than its non-resident output, and physics moved in step. The exception that matters is electrical engineering, where American doctorate production has not moved in fourteen years while non-resident production rose.

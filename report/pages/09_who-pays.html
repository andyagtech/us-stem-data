4. Who pays

The taxpayer-funded route through a doctorate is used disproportionately by visa holders

The Survey of Earned Doctorates asks every recipient how their doctoral study was primarily funded. Among science and engineering doctorate recipients in 2024, 76.0% of temporary-visa holders were supported principally by a research or teaching assistantship, against 53.1% of U.S. citizens and permanent residents.

The mirror of that gap is self-funding. U.S. citizens and permanent residents were 4.6 times more likely to pay their own way: 16.0% funded themselves from personal or family resources, against 3.5% of visa holders.

What that funding amounts to

Research assistantships are the single largest channel, and they are overwhelmingly financed by federal research awards. Across the 99 institutions awarding at least a hundred core-STEM research doctorates, federal obligations from all agencies came to a median of $874,630 for every research doctorate awarded.

That figure is an intensity ratio, not a price. Federal obligations fund research whose outputs include papers, instruments, facilities and staff, not only degrees, so it does not establish that the public paid $874,630 for any particular doctorate. What it does establish is the scale of public research money flowing through the institutions that produce them. The federal link behind the assistantship figures is likewise an inference, well supported rather than measured: the SED records that a student was funded by a research assistantship and never which award paid, while the graduate-student survey supplies the connection — 52,428 federally supported research assistantships against 1,041 teaching ones. No published field crosses funding agency with citizenship.

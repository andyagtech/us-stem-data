The same measures for every institution, on one identifier

The findings on pages {{page:funding}} and {{page:model}} each rest on a different survey with a different population. This puts them on one row for each of 2,290 institutions, joined on the federal unit identifier rather than the name, so any two can be compared and any group added to a state.

The join is exact or it is nothing. The two NCSES research surveys publish names and no identifier, and resolving those by similarity puts Florida A&M’s money in the University of Florida’s row. Names are matched exactly after normalisation or referred to a hand-checked list; 1.7% of R&D dollars and 3.1% of obligations stay unresolved and are excluded rather than guessed at — nearly all of them research institutes with no teaching unit, such as Scripps and Woods Hole. Across the 262 institutions nationally awarding enough core-STEM research doctorates to carry it, federal obligations run to a median of $821,054 for each awarded per year: a wider population than page {{page:whopays}}, and the same kind of number — an intensity ratio, not a price.

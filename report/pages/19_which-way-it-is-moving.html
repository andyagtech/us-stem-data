16. Which way it is moving

On the wage level, employers file higher than they did — the one measure in this document moving the other way

Two years cannot say whether a pattern is worsening, so the whole disclosure archive was read. The share of filings at the bottom two wage levels has fallen, from 80.5% in 2017 to 62.7% in 2025. Level I alone has fallen from 44.4% to 20.8%.

The break is a single year. Level I fell from 44.4% to 17.9% between 2017 and 2018 — the year after an executive order directed the agencies to scrutinise H-1B wage levels. Both files use the same column and the same visa-class field, so it is a real reading of the data and not a change in how it was recorded.

This is the level mix, and it is a different question from whether the wage clears the local market. Employers have moved up the legal scale while, on page {{page:wagelevels}}, the wage promised still sits below the median for the occupation in the local area in 84.0% of filings. Both are true: the scale itself sits low against local pay. The coincidence with the 2017 order is a coincidence in time, not a demonstrated cause, and the archive carries no wage level at all before 2017.

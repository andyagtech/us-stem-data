6. The discount

Equal pay is not equal cost: wages paid on OPT carry no payroll tax

Compared within a graduation cohort, doctorate holders trained on a temporary visa earn about what their American classmates earn: among those who finished in 2015 or later, a median of $103,000 against $100,000 for citizens trained here. That is a statement about salary, and salary is not what an employer pays. Wages earned on Optional Practical Training are exempt from Social Security and Medicare tax under IRC 3121(b)(19). The graduate is cheaper to employ at the same salary.

An American graduate competing for the same job carries 7.65% in employer payroll tax that the OPT graduate does not. The exemption is statutory and applies automatically — but it means the cost comparison an employer makes is not the wage comparison the salary data shows.

An authorisation is not a full-time year of employment, so applying an annual wage to a count overstates any single year. The exemption ends when the holder becomes a resident alien for tax purposes, generally after five calendar years in F or J status — so it is a discount on early-career employment, which is exactly the stage at which a new American graduate is competing.

7. Who holds the leverage

The employer files the petition, and the worker’s right to stay depends on keeping that job

The payroll-tax gap on page {{page:fica}} is a price difference. This is a bargaining difference, and it does not appear in any wage. A permanent-residence application is filed by the employer, for a specific job at a specific employer. Leaving generally means starting again.

An American doing the same job can leave on two weeks’ notice. A worker mid-process generally cannot without losing their place, and 74.6% of employment-based green cards go to people already here on a temporary status — so this is the normal condition of the employment route, not an edge case.

This page establishes the structure of that dependence: who files, on whose behalf, and from which status. It is not a measurement of what the dependence produces. No published dataset records whether a visa-holding worker accepted worse terms, worked longer hours, or declined to complain, and this document does not claim otherwise. Nor is a certification a green card: USCIS must still approve a petition and a visa number must be available, which is where the wait comes from.

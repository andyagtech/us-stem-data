14. Data needs, continued

Who is using the pipeline — and one ask that needs no new collection at all

5 · Graduate applications and admissions — NCES

IPEDS counts degrees conferred. Its Admissions component covers first-time undergraduates only, and no federal collection records who applied to graduate programmes, who was admitted, or who was offered funding. Would settle: whether domestic applicants are declining, being declined, or not applying — the question behind this document, and the one it cannot answer.

6 · L-1 and O-1 petitions by employer — USCIS

For H-1B this document can name the employer three times over: what it requested, what USCIS granted, what the Labor Department found. For L-1 and O-1 it cannot name the employer once. Neither classification requires a labour condition application, so the Labor Department never sees them, and USCIS publishes an employer data hub for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B but not for these. The closest substitute is the destination state declared on arrival — L-1 concentrates in Texas, California, New York and Michigan — which is a location, not an employer.

The specific ask. USCIS already holds this: the petitioner name and address on Form I-129 for classifications L-1A, L-1B, O-1A and O-1B, aggregated by petitioner and fiscal year with approvals and denials. That is the same field, from the same form and the same system, that produces the published H-1B Employer Data Hub — the extract exists for one classification and not the others. No new collection is required. Would settle: which companies transfer the most staff into the United States without a wage test, and whether O-1 growth sits in the industries most constrained by the H-1B cap.

7 · Student visas by institution — State and DHS

The State Department reports visa issuance by consulate and nationality and never by school; SEVP publishes school-level figures for OPT alone. So the institutions that bring in the most foreign students cannot be identified directly. What can be shown is who enrols them, from IPEDS, and who places the most into STEM OPT afterwards — two different populations, neither of which is an F-1 count. Would settle: where the student pipeline actually enters, which is the first stage of everything else in this document.

8. The other model

Where the students fund the institution rather than the taxpayer funding the research

Page {{page:funding}} follows the federal money. The institutions with the very highest master’s visa shares are somewhere else entirely: they receive little federal research money, and what they bill their foreign master’s students dwarfs it.

Harrisburg University of Science and Technology and the University of Central Missouri rank higher on share than anything listed above — 96.4% and 92.7% — and do not appear in the federal research survey at all.

Two different machines running on one visa. On page {{page:funding}} the taxpayer funds the research and the students work on it; here the students pay to be there. The tuition figure is sticker price, not receipts: gross of every waiver, remission and scholarship, and one year where a master’s normally runs one and a half to two. It measures the scale of the difference between the two models, not any institution’s income. The table is ordered by visa share rather than by the ratio, so institutions where tuition does not dominate appear too.

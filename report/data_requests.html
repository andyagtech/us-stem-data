Memorandum

Eight requests for federal data that is already collected and not published

To
The data-release and statistical offices named against each item
From
PhD Demographics project · phd-demographics.andy-barr.com
Date
11 September 2026
Subject
Aggregate extracts that would settle questions the published record cannot

This project reconstructs the U.S. graduate STEM pipeline — degrees, enrolment, funding, work authorisation and permanent residence — entirely from published federal data. At eight points the published record stops short. In seven of them the agency already collects the information and publishes it only in part, or not at all; in the eighth, graduate admissions, no federal collection exists.

Each request below names who holds the data, what is published now, and the smallest aggregate extract that would answer the question. Every request is for counts only, at the agency’s own disclosure thresholds — no person-level records. Each can be sent as it stands, as an agency data request, a FOIA request or through a congressional office.

The requests

1

H-1B cap-exempt status by employer

No new collection
Held by
USCIS
Collected on
Form I-129, which records whether a petition is cap-exempt and on what ground.
Published now
National totals by exemption category in the Report to Congress. The H-1B Employer Data Hub names every employer but carries no cap field.
Request
The Employer Data Hub extract with two added fields — cap-exempt (yes/no) and exemption ground — by employer and fiscal year, with approvals and denials.
Would settle
Which employers use the exemption, at what scale, and whether use is concentrated. Today the 24,073 petitions filed under the affiliated-nonprofit ground in FY2025 cannot be attributed to any organisation.
2

Federal graduate support by citizenship

No new collection
Held by
NSF National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics
Collected on
Survey of Graduate Students and Postdoctorates (support by mechanism and agency; enrolment by citizenship); Survey of Earned Doctorates (primary support by citizenship).
Published now
Support by agency, and enrolment by citizenship — never together. The SED records the assistantship but not which award paid for it. Federal money reaches doctoral students mainly through research assistantships: 52,428 federally supported, against 1,041 teaching assistantships.
Request
A tabulation of graduate students with federal support, by citizenship status (citizen or permanent resident; temporary visa) and by field, from existing returns. If the returns do not link the two, a statement from NCSES saying so, which would itself settle the question of whether the figure exists.
Would settle
What federal research funding spends per doctoral student by citizenship — turning a proxy into a measurement.
3

Citizens and permanent residents separated at conferral

No new collection
Held by
NSF NCSES
Collected on
Survey of Earned Doctorates, which records citizenship status at conferral in three categories.
Published now
Two categories: citizens and permanent residents combined, and temporary visa holders. The Survey of Doctorate Recipients separates them after graduation, but not at the degree. IPEDS does not collect the distinction at all; a permanent resident is counted under race and ethnicity.
Request
SED doctorate counts by field and year with U.S. citizens and permanent residents shown separately. Master’s degrees would need a change to IPEDS and are not part of this request.
Would settle
The citizen share of U.S. graduate STEM doctorates, which no published source reports.
4

OPT and STEM OPT for every institution

No new collection
Held by
DHS Student and Exchange Visitor Program
Collected on
SEVIS.
Published now
A Top 100 ranking of schools. A school outside it is absent rather than zero, so school totals are a floor and year-to-year movement may only reflect the cutoff. Complete school-level figures are released to school officials for their own institution, or by FOIA.
Request
OPT and STEM OPT authorisation counts for every certified school, by calendar year, on the same basis as the published national table.
Would settle
Work-authorisation participation for every institution, not just the hundred largest.
5

Graduate applications and admissions

Needs a new collection
Held by
No federal agency. The natural home is ED’s National Center for Education Statistics.
Collected on
Nothing. The IPEDS Admissions component covers first-time undergraduates only.
Published now
Degrees conferred. No federal collection records who applied to graduate programmes, who was admitted, or who was offered funding.
Request
A graduate admissions component in IPEDS: applications, admissions and funded offers by broad field and residency status. This is the one item on this list that asks for something new.
Would settle
Whether domestic applicants are declining, being declined, or not applying — the question behind the whole project, and the one it cannot answer.
6

L-1 and O-1 petitions by employer

No new collection
Held by
USCIS
Collected on
Form I-129, the same form and system that produce the published H-1B Employer Data Hub.
Published now
Nothing by employer. Neither classification requires a labor condition application, so the Labor Department never sees them, and USCIS publishes employer data hubs for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B only. The closest substitute is the destination state declared on arrival, which is a location, not an employer.
Request
Petitioner name and address for classifications L-1A, L-1B, O-1A and O-1B, aggregated by petitioner and fiscal year, with approvals and denials — the H-1B hub’s extract, run for four more classifications.
Would settle
Which companies transfer the most staff into the United States without a wage test, and whether O-1 growth sits in the industries most constrained by the H-1B cap.
7

F-1 students by institution

No new collection
Held by
DHS SEVP; the State Department for visa issuance
Collected on
SEVIS, which records every F-1 student against a certified school.
Published now
State reports issuance by consulate and nationality, never by school. SEVP publishes school-level figures for OPT alone. IPEDS reports non-resident enrolment, a residency category rather than a visa count.
Request
Active F-1 student records by certified school and year, split by education level (bachelor’s, master’s, doctorate).
Would settle
Where the student pipeline enters — the first stage of everything else in this memo.
8

Country of birth for nonimmigrant classes

No new collection
Held by
State Department (visa issuance); DHS (admissions)
Collected on
The nonimmigrant visa application, which records place of birth alongside nationality.
Published now
Every visa and admission series is by nationality. A naturalised citizen of one country born in another cannot be distinguished from a native-born citizen anywhere in the published data.
Request
Nonimmigrant visa issuances by visa class, fiscal year, nationality and country of birth. For classes admitted without a visa — TN status for Canadian citizens is the main case — the equivalent from DHS admission records, if they carry birthplace; which is for DHS to confirm.
Would settle
How far a nationality series reflects the country people come from, and whether routes such as TN are used by nationals born elsewhere.

None of these extracts would identify a person. Each asks for counts at the agency’s standard suppression thresholds, and where a cell falls below them, the suppressed cell is itself a useful answer.

Every figure in this memo is taken from the project’s published datasets: H-1B cap-exempt filings from USCIS’s FY2025 H-1B petitions report, support by mechanism from the NSF GSS. The site and its sources are at phd-demographics.andy-barr.com/data_sources.html.

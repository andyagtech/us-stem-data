# Definitions that are easy to conflate

## NRA is a visa status, not an ethnicity and not a country

IPEDS "non-resident alien" (renamed "U.S. Nonresident" in the 2023
collection year, same students) is a **reported residency/visa status**. IPEDS defines
it as a person "who is not a citizen or national of the United States and who is
in this country on a visa or temporary basis and does not have the right to
remain indefinitely."

So the category means **here on a temporary visa**. For students that is
overwhelmingly F-1 and J-1.

**A green card holder is never NRA.** Nor is a refugee or an asylee. Those
students are reported in the race and ethnicity categories alongside citizens,
which is why NRA is not a count of "foreign students" in the everyday sense -- a
permanent resident who grew up abroad is not in it.

The status is recorded **when the student enrols** for enrolment figures and
**when the degree is awarded** for degree counts. It says nothing about
ethnicity, ancestry, or country of origin. A US-born citizen of any background is
never NRA.

If a reader slides from "non-resident alien" to a nationality or an ethnic group,
correct it plainly once, then answer the question they meant.

## Name-origin classification is a separate, weaker signal

Department-level breakdowns and dissertation records on this site use a
**name-origin classification** — an estimate of likely origin from an author's
name. It is not visa status and must never be presented as such. Always say which
of the two you are quoting.

A study on this corpus found that names add very little once field of study is
known: the incremental explanatory power over field alone is small, and smaller
still once the published NRA share is included, with typical errors of roughly ten
percentage points and the weakest performance in Engineering. The raw correlation
is real and moderately strong — the signal does **not** vanish — but what it mostly
encodes is **field composition**, not visa status. Never offer name-based
estimation as a substitute for IPEDS, and never present it as a visa measure.

## Two STEM definitions exist in this project and they disagree

- **Narrow, 8 CIP families** — 11, 14, 15, 26, 27, 30, 40, 42. This drives every
  **headline NRA share** on the site and on the share cards.
- **Broad, 16 CIP families** — adds 01, 03, 04, 10, 29, 41, 45, 51 (agriculture,
  health, social sciences and others). This drives the **per-university
  completions chart**.

They usually differ by only a point or two, but for some institutions the gap is
large enough to change the story entirely. If a number you state could come from
either basis, say which one it uses — and if a reader quotes a figure that does
not match yours, the basis is the first thing to check.

"Non-STEM" on this site means the complement of the **broad** set — education,
humanities, social sciences. The narrow complement would sweep health and
agriculture in with them and misdescribe the pages.

## The pinned basis for headline shares

Headline per-university NRA shares are computed over **CIP families 11, 14, 15,
26, 27, 30, 40, 42**, years **2015-2022 inclusive**, **award level 17**. All three
parts matter; changing any one changes the number.

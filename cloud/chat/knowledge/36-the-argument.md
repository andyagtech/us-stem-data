# The argument this site makes, in four steps

## Why this file exists

`01-central-finding.md` states the empirical finding: concentration, not a
rising national share. This file states the **argument built on top of it** --
the case the homepage makes and the order the left rail now follows. A reader
who asks "what is this site saying?" or "why does this matter?" wants this
file. A reader who asks "what does the data show?" wants `01`.

Keep them apart. The finding is measured. The argument is an
argument, and readers may reject it while accepting every figure in it.

## The four steps

The homepage leads with these four, numbered, and the sidebar sections carry
the same numbers so the two surfaces agree.

### 1. The public pays for the doctorate

A doctorate in science and engineering is not bought, it is funded: an
assistantship, tuition waived, a stipend, and the work paid for largely out of
federal research grants. **About $875,000 of federal research funding per STEM
doctorate awarded.** An American doctoral student is **more than four times as
likely to be paying their own way** than a non-resident one -- 16.0% against
3.5%.

Pages: `/ipeds_dashboard`, `/distributions`, `/non_stem`,
`/rankings`. The figures live on `/#public-cost`.

### 2. The master's is sold

The master's is the opposite transaction. It is a cash business, and it grew.
**About 60% of new core-STEM master's seats went to non-residents -- roughly
96,000 of 159,000 -- over a decade in which American master's numbers barely
moved.** Every seat is one an American applicant did not get.

Pages: `/expansion`, `/institutions`, `/rankings`.

### 3. The degree becomes a visa

The degree is also a work permit. **194,554 students were on OPT in 2024, 2.5
times the 77,619 of 2014.** Two things follow, and both are written in federal
law rather than left to the market:

- **No payroll tax.** 26 U.S.C. 3121(b)(19) excludes the wages from
  "employment", so neither the employer's 7.65% nor the worker's 7.65% is due.
  The employer's cost of a given take-home pay is lower.
- **The job is the visa.** Status depends on employment: 90 days of
  unemployment ends it, 150 across the STEM extension. A graduate on OPT cannot
  leave a bad employer the way an American graduate can.

Pages: `/immigration`, `/pipeline`, `/workforce`,
`/medical`, `/filers`, and `/visa-schools#cheaper` for the cost
argument in full.

### 4. Who profits

**57 US colleges where foreign students are most of the graduate enrolment**,
up from 43 in fall 2022; seven are 90% foreign or more. Between them they took
**$1.0bn in federal student aid**, and the nonprofits among them reported
**$89.7M paid to recruiting firms**. Downstream, a staffing trade sponsors
those graduates and rents them to other companies: see
`37-the-body-shops.md`.

Pages: `/visa-schools`, `/bodyshops`, `/usaid`, `/filers`.

## The wording, which is deliberate

The site says schools **"launder visas"** and calls the firms **"body shops"**.
Both are the author's chosen words and both stay. Neither is a legal finding,
and you must not upgrade either into one: no page here says any school or firm
has broken a law, and you must not say it either. What the pages show is what
public records show. If a reader challenges the wording, say plainly that it is
the site's characterisation, then give them the records underneath it.

## Different readers, different paths

The rail can only encode one route. Five run through this material, and a
reader who is on the wrong one should be moved rather than argued with:

1. **The case** -- the four steps above, in order. The default.
2. **The route** -- one person's journey: F-1, degree, OPT, H-1B, green card,
   workforce. Same pages, read as chronology. `/pipeline` is the spine.
3. **The lookup** -- "what about my university?" Send them to
   `/universities/{key}`, `/institutions` or `/rankings`, and
   use `list_universities` for keys. This is a search, not an argument.
4. **The comparison** -- `/india`, `/china`, `/canada`,
   `/uk`, `/eu`, `/mexico`, `/vwp`.
5. **The audit** -- "prove it". `/data_sources`, `/methodology`,
   `/quality`, `/data_requests`, `/query`. Send anyone who
   challenges the data here rather than defending numbers in chat.
   `/data_requests` is the Data Asks page: what a federal office holds, or
   once published, that would answer a question this site cannot. Send a
   reader there when the honest answer is that the data does not exist. Most
   of the asks need no new collection.

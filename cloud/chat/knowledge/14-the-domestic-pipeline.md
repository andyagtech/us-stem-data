# The domestic pipeline: output, conversion, and what degrees pay

The question behind the site is whether America trains enough of its own STEM
talent. The intuitive version of the answer is wrong, and saying so first is
what makes the rest credible.

## Domestic output is rising, at every level

IPEDS Completions, eight core-STEM CIP families, domestic recipients:

- bachelor's **399,752 (2011) → 624,699 (2025), +56%**
- master's **85,451 → 155,921, +82%**
- research doctorates **19,059 → 25,794, +35%**

If asked whether America produces enough STEM graduates, lead with these. A
claim that domestic production is collapsing is refuted by three numbers
anyone can pull from IPEDS.

## What changed is where they stop

Against a lagged bachelor's cohort — two years to a master's, six to a
doctorate:

- bachelor's → master's **23.88% → 26.63%** (rising)
- **bachelor's → doctorate 5.41% → 4.70%** (falling, about an eighth)

Americans finish STEM bachelor's degrees in growing numbers and continue to a
master's at a rising rate. The share going on to a doctorate has fallen. That
is the defensible version of "the pipeline is broken", and it is narrow.

The lag is a modelling choice. IPEDS counts **degrees, not people**, so this is
a ratio between two populations rather than a tracked cohort. It cannot say
whether someone chose industry, was not admitted, or was not funded — no
federal source records graduate applications or admissions at all.

## By field, including the fields that disagree

Doctorates, 2011 → 2025, domestic and non-resident:

- **electrical engineering: domestic 693 → 695 (flat for fourteen years)**, NRA +9%
- statistics: domestic 155 → 142 (−8%), NRA +97%
- physics: domestic +25%, NRA +25% — in step
- **mechanical engineering: domestic +68%, NRA +58% — domestic grew faster**
- computer science: domestic +94%, NRA +209%

Electrical engineering is the strongest single case. Mechanical engineering
runs against the pattern and should be said aloud rather than left to be
found.

## What the degrees pay

College Scorecard, median earnings one year after completion, by field and
credential level.

- median doctorate premium over a bachelor's **in the same field: +99%**
- in **3 of 27** fields the doctorate pays less than the master's: engineering
  general, geosciences, physics

Do **not** compare the pooled medians across levels. Suppression leaves 208
doctoral programmes against 7,121 bachelor's and they are a different mix of
fields, which is why the pooled doctoral figure lands below the pooled
master's when only 3 of 27 do so within a field.

And this is not a return to the degree: each figure is measured one year after
that credential finishes, so the bachelor's cohort is about 23 and the
doctoral cohort about 29 — the bachelor's holder has six years of earnings the
comparison does not count.

## Who the degrees go to

IPEDS records citizenship and race in **one field with nine mutually exclusive
values**. Non-resident sits beside the race categories, not above them: a
permanent resident is counted under their race, and a foreign national on a
student visa is counted as non-resident whatever their race.

Core-STEM master's degrees, 2025: non-resident 45.9%, White 26.4%, Asian 8.3%,
Hispanic 7.8%, Black 5.5%.

Because non-residents are in that denominator, a group's share of **all**
degrees falls when non-resident numbers rise even if its own count is
unchanged. White recipients as a share of degrees **to residents only** went
60.9% (2011) → 48.7% (2025); as a share of **all** degrees, 43.4% → 26.4%.
Comparisons against national population shares need the resident-only figure.

The data cannot separate citizens from permanent residents. "Domestic"
throughout means both together.

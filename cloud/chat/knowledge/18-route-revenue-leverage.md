# The route in, the revenue, and who holds the leverage

Three findings that sit behind the degree counts. Use them when someone asks
what the visa numbers actually mean.

## 1. The employment green card converts people already here

DHS splits every class of permanent residence into people who arrived from
abroad and people already in the US who adjusted status without leaving.

| Class, FY2023 | Total | Adjusted from inside | New arrival |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment-based | 196,760 | 74.6% | 25.4% |
| Immediate relatives | 551,590 | 57.3% | 42.7% |
| Family-sponsored | 204,240 | 5.4% | 94.6% |
| Diversity lottery | 67,350 | 1.4% | 98.6% |

**74.6% of employment-based green cards went to people already in the
country** (146,880 against
49,890). It has run between
74.6% and 91.6% since 2014.

The contrast carries the point: family-sponsored admission and the diversity
lottery bring people in from outside. The employment route mostly changes the
status of people already living and working here.

**Limit:** an adjustment shows the person was already in the country. It does
**not** record which status they held before, so this cannot say they studied
here. PERM does carry that field and points the same way --
57.6% of labour certifications are filed for
someone on an H-1B, 7.9% for someone on a
student visa.

## 2. The master's is the level that pays

A doctoral student is normally paid to be there. A master's student normally
is not: GSS puts master's assistantship rates at 7.0% RA and 6.9% TA, so about
**86% hold neither**. That is why the balance moved at master's level.

Published tuition billed to non-resident core-STEM master's graduates rose from
$0.85bn to **$3.36bn**
a year in constant dollars, up 297% real, on
97,850 degrees.

**Limit:** this is **sticker price, not receipts** -- published out-of-state
tuition times degrees conferred, not net of discounts, waivers or aid, and not
what any institution banked. Never call it revenue. A master's runs 1.5-2
years, so a whole-programme figure is larger than the annual one.

## 3. Leverage is structural, and it is not measured

A permanent-residence application is filed **by the employer**, for a specific
job at a specific employer; leaving generally means starting over.
57.6% of certifications are for someone already
working for that employer on an H-1B, and 45.8%
are for Indian nationals, who face the longest wait for a visa number.

**Say what this is and is not.** It establishes the *structure* of dependence:
who files, on whose behalf, from which status. **No published dataset records
whether a visa-holding worker accepted worse terms, worked longer hours, or
declined to complain.** Do not assert the consequence as if it were measured.
A certification is also not a green card -- USCIS must still approve a petition
and a visa number must be available.

## 4. Enrolment corroborates the degree series

IPEDS Fall Enrolment, **all fields, not core STEM**: full-time graduate
enrolment went 14.5% to
23.1% non-resident, all graduate
10.4% to 17.1%.

**Limit:** all fields, so it is **not comparable** with the core-STEM degree
counts and must never be quoted beside them as if it were. It also runs about
two years behind completions, so it corroborates rather than leads.

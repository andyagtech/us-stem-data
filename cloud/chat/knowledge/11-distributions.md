# The distributions, and what they are for

`/distributions` takes the national average apart three ways. When a reader
asks why a flat national number matters, send them here rather than explaining
it in prose — the shapes carry the argument.

## 1. The whole distribution, then and now

Every department in the country bucketed by non-resident share. A department is
one 4-digit CIP programme at one institution with 20+ research doctorates that
year.

    2010   227 departments   median 38.1%    73 above 50%   23 above 70%
    2015   308               median 40.0%   115            40
    2020   330               median 45.7%   144            72
    2025   425               median 40.7%   162            67

The top bins thicken markedly — above 70% nearly tripled, and the 80–90% band
went from 5 departments to 23 — while the median moved barely two points. That
is the flat-average-hiding-a-distribution claim, drawn.

Note that 2020 is the peak and 2025 is below it. Do not present the series as
monotonic; the honest shape is a rise, a peak, and an easing.

The lowest bin includes institutions that filed a total but left the residency
breakdown blank, so it is an upper bound on genuinely domestic programmes rather
than a count of them.

## 2. Which programmes, not which universities

Pooled across every institution, 2015–2022, programmes with 300+ doctorates
nationally. The range is enormous and stable:

    85.3%  Petroleum Engineering
    71.4%  Electrical & Electronics Engineering   17,930 doctorates, 162 schools
    67.2%  Statistics
    63.1%  Computer Science
    ...
     3.5%  Clinical Psychology
     2.5%  Computer & Information Systems Security
     1.5%  Applied Behavior Analysis

This is a property of **fields**, not of particular universities, and that is
the useful framing: a reader who thinks one school is unusual is usually
noticing its field mix.

The security line is worth noting if a reader asks about computing. Information security sits at 2.5% while general computer science sits at 63.1%. The most plausible reading is that work close to classified programmes is largely closed to people on temporary visas. State it as a reading. IPEDS records the degree, not why anyone was admitted, and 511 doctorates is a small base.

Do not say the field "requires citizenship": no field of study does, and the rules that do apply to particular work admit lawful permanent residents. The distinction is set out in `38-critical-industries.md`, and it is a footnote rather than an argument.

## 3. Inside one university

A single share per university misleads and should be corrected when a reader
leans on one. MIT is not "43.8% non-resident" in any useful sense: it runs from
15.8% in biology to 73.2% in computer science, and the average describes none of
its departments.

    Purdue        20 of 27 departments majority non-resident, top 82.3%
    Stony Brook   12 of 24, computer engineering at 100%
    MIT           11 of 27, range 15.8%–73.2%

Widest internal spreads — Stony Brook 100 points, Boston University 97.8,
Houston 97.5 — are where a headline share misleads most.

Programmes need 15+ doctorates over 2015–2022 to appear, so the count shown is
smaller than the institution's full programme list.

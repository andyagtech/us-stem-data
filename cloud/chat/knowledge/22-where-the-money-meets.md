## Use the DOCTORAL share, not the master's

This is the correction that matters most here. Federal research money pays
**research assistantships**, and GSS shows master's students are overwhelmingly
unfunded (about 86% hold no assistantship). So a high *master's* visa share at
a well-funded institution says nothing about where the grant money goes.

Worked example: **Saint Louis University** is 88.9% foreign at master's level
on 2,637 students, with $44m of federal R&D -- which invites the reading that
federal money is funding foreign students. Its **doctoral** share is 35.5%,
*below* the 44.5% national average. The federal money there is not reaching a
mostly foreign doctoral population; the foreign concentration is in the
tuition-paying master's programme. Georgia Tech (55.1% doctoral, $1,213m) is
the case that actually fits.

**Further limits:** HERD is R&D **expenditure**, not all federal money and not
student support. It is recorded at the institution -- **nothing traces a dollar
to a named student**. Say "federal money is at these institutions", never
"federal money was paid to these students".

## The mechanism behind India's H-1B concentration

India is 68.6% of H-1B issuances but only 21.5% of F-1 -- about 1.2x its
population share among students, the same as China. Where does the H-1B
concentration come from? Compare the two flows in the same year:

| Country | H-1B issuances | F-1 issuances | H-1B per F-1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Philippines | 3,562 | 1,020 | **3.49** |
| India | 150,647 | 86,067 | **1.75** |
| China - mainland | 31,735 | 82,654 | **0.38** |
| Pakistan | 1,664 | 4,483 | **0.37** |
| Great Britain and Northern Ireland | 1,462 | 4,655 | **0.31** |
| Mexico | 1,954 | 6,422 | **0.3** |
| Taiwan | 1,830 | 7,365 | **0.25** |
| Brazil | 1,801 | 7,431 | **0.24** |

**India issues 1.75 H-1B visas per F-1 visa.** More people arrive on the work
visa each year than on the student visa, which the US student pipeline cannot
account for -- consistent with hiring from abroad rather than conversion from
US study. China is 0.38, and everyone else 0.24-0.37.

**Confound to raise first:** visas are **re-stamped for travel**, so a worker
who goes home and returns generates another issuance. That inflates the
numerator for nationalities whose workers travel most, and this is **not a
conversion rate** -- it compares two flows in one year and tracks nobody
between them.

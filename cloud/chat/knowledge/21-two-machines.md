# Two machines, one visa

## 1. The federal money and the enrolment are in the same institutions

Institutions sorted into quartiles by federal R&D expenditure (HERD FY2024),
against their temporary-visa share of graduate enrolment (GSS 2024):

| Quartile | Median federal R&D | Doctoral visa share | Doctoral students | Master's visa share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | $3m | 20.5% | 7,631 | 32.9% |
| Q2 | $19m | 44.2% | 15,520 | 35.8% |
| Q3 | $90m | 49.0% | 49,885 | 50.9% |
| Q4 | $454m | 43.6% | 167,396 | 50.3% |

The best-funded quartile holds 167,396 doctoral students at
43.6% visa share; the least-funded holds 7,631 at
20.5%. **The public money and the foreign enrolment are not in
different places.**

## 2. The other machine: tuition, not grants

The highest master's *shares* are somewhere else entirely -- institutions
where the students fund the institution rather than the taxpayer funding the
research:

| Institution | Master's visa share | Tuition billed | Federal R&D | Tuition per $1 R&D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Illinois Institute of Technology | 94.1% | $67m | $32m | **2.11x** |
| Clark University | 92.3% | $32m | $3m | **10.05x** |
| University of Arkansas at Little Rock | 90.9% | $4m | $10m | **0.38x** |
| Saint Louis University | 88.9% | $57m | $44m | **1.31x** |
| Central Michigan University | 88.7% | $13m | $12m | **1.17x** |
| Purdue University Northwest | 88.3% | $2m | $4m | **0.52x** |
| University of North Texas | 87.0% | $59m | $43m | **1.36x** |
| Pace University | 86.6% | $35m | $2m | **16.93x** |

Harrisburg University and the University of Central Missouri rank higher on
share than anything here and **do not appear in the R&D survey at all**.

**Both are real and they must not be merged.** In the quartile table the
taxpayer funds the research and the students work on it. In this table the
students pay to be there. The same visa runs both.

**Limits:** tuition is one year of *published* out-of-state graduate tuition
and fees times the foreign master's head count -- **sticker price, gross of
every waiver and scholarship**, and annual where a master's normally runs
longer. Never call it revenue. HERD is R&D expenditure, not all federal money.
172 institutions could not be name-matched, and **unmatched
does not mean unfunded**.

## 3. Overrepresentation is at the work stage, not the student stage

Share of issuances against share of world population (World Bank, 2024):

**F-1 students**

| Country | F-1 share | Population share | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| India | 21.5% | 17.82% | **1.2x** |
| China - mainland | 20.6% | 17.31% | **1.2x** |
| Vietnam | 4.6% | 1.24% | **3.7x** |
| Korea, South | 3.7% | 0.64% | **5.8x** |
| Nepal | 3.3% | 0.36% | **9.1x** |
| Japan | 2.2% | 1.52% | **1.4x** |
| France | 1.9% | 0.84% | **2.3x** |
| Brazil | 1.9% | 2.6% | **0.7x** |
| Italy | 1.6% | 0.72% | **2.2x** |

**H-1B**

| Country | H-1B share | Population share | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| India | 68.6% | 17.82% | **3.8x** |
| China - mainland | 14.4% | 17.31% | **0.8x** |
| Philippines | 1.6% | 1.42% | **1.1x** |
| Korea, South | 1.0% | 0.64% | **1.6x** |
| Mexico | 0.9% | 1.61% | **0.6x** |
| Brazil | 0.8% | 2.6% | **0.3x** |

India and China enter the student pipeline almost identically -- both about
1.2x their population share. They diverge completely at the work visa: India
**3.8x**,
China 0.8x, which is *below* its population share. Nepal and South Korea are
far more overrepresented than India among students; India's concentration is
specifically at H-1B.

**Limits:** population is one defensible denominator among several (share of
the world's STEM graduates would be another) and the ratio is labelled as
against population only. Taiwan has no World Bank population record and is
therefore absent from these tables -- see `no_population_record`.

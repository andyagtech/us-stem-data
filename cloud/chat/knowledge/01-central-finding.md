# The central finding

The headline of this site is **not** that the non-resident share of US doctorates
is rising nationally. It is not. Asserting a rising national share is the single
most common way to get this site's argument wrong.

The shape of the finding, which you may state without a tool:

- The **national NRA share of US research doctorates is roughly flat** across the
  post-2010 period, drifting slightly downward.
- What changed is **concentration**. The number of departments awarding 20 or more
  doctorates that are majority non-resident has more than doubled, and the share
  of all US doctorates awarded inside those departments has risen substantially.
- The effect is markedly stronger in STEM than across all fields.

The claim is about **distribution, not volume**. Whole departments became majority
non-resident while the national average held still.

**Fetch the magnitudes; do not recall them.** Call `get_national_series` for the
national line and `nra_lookup` for any share, count or year. Figures on this site
are recomputed as the corpus grows, so any number you remember is a number that
can already be wrong. State the shape from here; state the values from a tool.

## The 2010 discontinuity — never straddle it

IPEDS recoded award levels in 2010. Legacy level 9 ("doctor's degree") was split
into 17 (research/scholarship) and 18 (professional practice). A pre-2010 figure
compared against a post-2010 figure measures the recoding, not the world.

Quote the **STEM series**, which is built consistently across the break, or stay
**within the post-2010 series**. Never one against the other. If a reader asks
about change since the 1990s, give them the STEM series and say why.

# Tuition, net price and state support, 2011-2023

## The window decides the answer

Two different stories live in this data, and quoting one without its dates
misrepresents the other.

**2008-2012 — the substitution.** University of Washington in-state tuition
rose 70.7% in real terms while Washington's appropriations per student fell
35.8%. In 2008 the state contributed more than a student paid ($10,378 against
$9,910); by 2012 the student paid $16,919 and the state $6,659.

**2011-2023 — the recovery, and it runs the other way.**

| Measure | Median real change | States that fell |
|---|---|---|
| In-state sticker | +2.5% | 21/51 |
| Out-of-state sticker | -3.7% | 35/51 |
| Net price (what aided students pay) | -6.6% | 35/51 |

Median state appropriations per FTE: **$8,759 in 2011 to
$9,741 in 2023, +11.2% real.**

So since 2011 states have put in **more** per student, and what aided students
actually pay has **fallen** in real terms in 35 of 51 states. "Students pay
more while states pay less" describes the crash years, not the decade after
them. **Always give the window.**

## Sticker, net price and the non-resident differential

Three different numbers, University of Washington 2023:

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| In-state published tuition and fees | $13,016 |
| **Out-of-state published** | **$43,236** |
| Net price, aided in-state undergraduate | $11,348 |

The out-of-state rate is **3.3x** the in-state rate. That differential is what
makes a non-resident — foreign or domestic — financially valuable to a public
institution, and it is the mechanism behind the tuition findings elsewhere in
this project.

**Net price is a different measure, not a corrected sticker.** It covers
first-time full-time undergraduates receiving aid, and it counts cost of
attendance including living costs minus grant aid. It is the best published
answer to "what does someone actually pay" and it is not comparable
line-for-line with the published rate beside it.

## Limits

- Published rates are **sticker before any discount, waiver or aid**.
- Two series moving apart is a **pattern, not a demonstrated cause**:
  legislatures set appropriations and governing boards set tuition, both
  responding to enrolment, cost and politics at once.
- Public institutions only. Constant 2024 dollars — CPI-U for tuition,
  SHEEO's HECA index for appropriations.
- 1,740 institutions for rates, 1,857
  for net price.

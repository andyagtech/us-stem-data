# L-1 and O-1: what exists, and what does not

## What does not exist

**There is no employer-level data for L-1 or O-1.** Neither classification
requires a labour condition application, so the Labor Department never sees
them. USCIS publishes an employer data hub for H-1B, H-2A and H-2B — not for
these. So "which companies bring in the most L-1s" **has no published
answer**, and neither does the O-1 equivalent.

State that plainly when asked. Do **not** substitute H-1B employer figures as
if they covered these classes.

### The specific ask, if someone in government can act on it

USCIS already holds this. The petitioner name and address on **Form I-129**
for classifications **L-1A, L-1B, O-1A and O-1B**, aggregated by petitioner
and fiscal year with approvals and denials. That is the same field, from the
same form and the same system, that produces the published H-1B Employer Data
Hub — the extract exists for one classification and not the others. **No new
collection is required.**

**F-1 issuance by university does not exist either.** State reports visas by
consulate and nationality, never by school; SEVP publishes school-level
figures for OPT alone. What can be shown is who *enrols* foreign students
(IPEDS) and who places graduates into STEM OPT (SEVP) — different populations,
neither an F-1 count.

Both are now on the briefing's Data Needs page as items 6 and 7.

## What does exist: admissions

| Class | FY2014 | FY2023 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-1B specialty occupation | 511,770 | 755,020 | +48% |
| L-1 intracompany transferee | 591,730 | 503,490 | -15% |
| O-1 extraordinary ability | 83,000 | 108,180 | +30% |
| F-1 student | 1,737,930 | 1,625,740 | -6% |
| J-1 exchange visitor | 486,830 | 481,280 | -1% |
| TN professional | 712,740 | 1,204,910 | +69% |

**L-1 admissions fell 15% while H-1B rose 48%** and TN rose 69%. The
intracompany route is not the one that grew.

Nearest thing to an employer signal — the destination state declared on
arrival:

| Class | Leading destinations |
|---|---|
| L-1 intracompany transferee | Texas 73,350, California 62,580, New York 62,070, Michigan 42,080, Florida 31,490 |
| O-1 extraordinary ability | New York 31,260, California 30,200, Florida 11,200, Texas 3,880, Massachusetts 2,880 |
| H-1B specialty occupation | California 139,080, Michigan 77,910, New York 74,680, Texas 72,450, Washington 70,390 |

L-1 concentrating in Michigan alongside the usual three is the automotive
pattern; O-1 in New York and California is entertainment and technology. **A
state is a location, not an employer.**

## The measure trap

**An admission is an entry at a port, not a person and not a visa.** One
worker travelling four times is four admissions. That is why FY2023 shows
755,020 H-1B admissions against 265,777 H-1B issuances, and 1,625,740 F-1
admissions against 445,418 issuances.

**Never mix the two series.** Issuances count documents, admissions count
arrivals, and neither counts workers. Quoting an admissions figure as if it
were a headcount overstates by a factor of three or more.

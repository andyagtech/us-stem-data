# Postdocs and citizenship

A postdoctoral appointment is the job an American doctorate leads to. It sits
between the degree and a faculty post or a research career. About half of
these positions are paid for with federal research money.

The page is `/postdocs`.

## The measured finding

58.3% of postdoctoral appointees in the United States hold a temporary visa.
American citizens and permanent residents are 41.7%.

Americans are a minority of the postdoctoral workforce. That is true
nationally, not at a handful of institutions.

The source is the NSF/NCSES Survey of Graduate Students and Postdoctorates,
2024. It covers 69,877 postdocs at 339 institutions.

## What "foreign" means here

A postdoc counted as foreign holds a temporary visa. A US citizen counts as
American and so does a permanent resident. That is the stricter reading: it
does not inflate the foreign share by counting green-card holders.

The two categories sum to the total exactly. The builder asserts this and
fails if it ever stops being true.

## The nine named universities

On 8 October 2026 the Department of Labor and the Department of Justice
announced an investigation into J-1 visa use at nine universities: Harvard,
Yale, Stanford, Brown, the University of Pittsburgh, UC Davis, Caltech,
Arizona State and MIT.

The figure given in public was that about 61% of postdoctoral researchers and
federally funded workers at those institutions are foreign nationals, against
a national average of 38%.

Checked against the NSF survey:

- **The 61% is right.** The nine come to 60.7%, 8,545 of 14,080 postdocs.
- **The national average is 58.3% on the same measure, not 38%.**

So the nine sit about 2.4 points above the national figure. They are not
outliers. They look much like the rest of the country.

This is the more important half and it must not be dropped. Without it the
finding reads as nine unusual universities, and the survey does not show
that. What it shows is a national postdoctoral workforce in which Americans
are a minority everywhere.

The 38% figure may count a different population. Postdocs, graduate students
and "federally funded workers" are three different groups with three
different shares. Do not assume which one was meant.

## Postdocs are not graduate students

These are separate populations and their shares differ by about fourteen
points.

- Postdocs: 58.3% foreign.
- Doctoral students: 44.5% foreign (the GSS institution ranking).

Never substitute one for the other, and never quote one figure as if it
answered a question about the other.

## Federal support

48.1% of postdoctoral appointments are federally supported: 33,582 posts.

**The survey reports how many postdocs are federally supported and how many
are foreign. It does not report how many are both.** Those two figures cannot
be multiplied. A question about how many foreign postdocs are on federal money
cannot be answered from this data, and the honest answer is that the survey
does not cross those fields.

## No legal claim

The page reports that an investigation was announced, on what date, and what
the survey counts. It does not say any university broke a law.

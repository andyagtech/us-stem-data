# The thesis and dissertation name lists

These are the most persuasive material on the site and the pages most worth
sending a reader to. The entry point is `/name-lists`.

Every other page reports a share or a count. These report the research
itself: 708,393 doctoral and master's theses from 106 American universities,
1966 to 2026, each with the author's name, the department, the year and the
title of the work.

Every name links to the awarding university's own repository, so any single
record can be checked at its source. That is what makes these lists
different from a chart: nothing has to be taken on trust.

## Where they are

- `/name-lists` is the entry point: what the lists are, how to read one, and
  the two doors into them.
- `/stem` holds 509,072 STEM records.
- `/non_stem` holds 199,321 non-STEM records.
- `/universities/<key>` is one university's own list.

## How a reader uses them

Pick a university, expand a department, expand a year, read the names and
titles. The department is the right unit. A university-wide figure averages a
physics department against a school of education; the department list shows
one field, one year at a time.

Suggest these whenever someone asks what the research actually looks like,
who is doing it, what fields are affected, or wants to verify a claim about a
particular institution. If a reader is sceptical of a share or a chart, send
them here: the underlying records are individually checkable.

## What the record is not

**No university is required to publish any of this.** There is no federal
rule obliging an institution to post a dissertation or to list the ones it
has awarded, and there is no national register. Each university decides, and
the decision is often revisited department by department.

The record was therefore built one institution at a time. Coverage is a
floor, not a total. A university that appears smaller here may simply publish
less, so two institutions must not be ranked against each other on record
count alone.

Of the 106 institutions searched, 7 publish nothing the harvest could find
and 27 more are reachable only through a handle or DOI resolver rather than a
browsable index. `/data_requests#no-requirement` sets out the ask that would
fix this.

## The names themselves

Names are reproduced as the awarding repository recorded them, with catalogue
artefacts removed where they are unambiguous: a degree or an institution glued
onto the author field, an occupational gloss, a life-dates suffix.

Where a suffix could be part of a real name it is deliberately left alone.
"M.S.", "M.A." and "S. M." are shaped exactly like middle initials and in many
names that is what they are, so they are never stripped. A tidy wrong name
would be worse than an untidy right one on pages whose whole value is that
they name real people.

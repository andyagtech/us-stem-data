# Who pays for a doctorate

This is the part of the site that is about **allocation** rather than
composition. Everything else measures who received the degrees; this measures
who was paid to be there. It is the strongest evidence on the site and also the
easiest to overstate, so the limits below are not optional.

## The finding

NSF Survey of Earned Doctorates, Table 4-1, 2024, science and engineering:

| Primary source of support | US citizen / PR | Temporary visa |
|---|---|---|
| Research assistantship | 35.4% | **53.6%** |
| Teaching assistantship | 17.7% | 22.4% |
| **Assistantships combined** | **53.1%** | **76.0%** |
| Fellowship or scholarship | 27.6% | 17.4% |
| **Own resources** | **16.0%** | **3.5%** |

Three in four international science and engineering doctorate recipients are
supported by an assistantship. Barely half of American ones are. Americans are
about **four and a half times** more likely to be paying their own way.

**The gap appears in every field** — nineteen of nineteen, from +11.8 points in
the social sciences to +25.9 in interdisciplinary science. Computer science
runs +23.9 with citizens self-funding at 5.5 times the rate; physical sciences
+12.5 at 6.0 times. Not one field runs the other way. Say this when someone
suggests it is a quirk of one discipline.

## Why the survey is hard to dismiss

The SED is a **census, not a sample**. Essentially every research doctorate
recipient in the United States completes it before graduating, and institutions
are required to administer it. It is the same instrument NSF uses to produce the
official national count of doctorates.

## The scale of federal money

NSF Graduate Students and Postdoctorates Survey, 2024:

- 274,601 full-time doctoral students; **67,936 (24.7%)** have federal support
  as their primary source
- **52,428** federally funded research assistants
- **46% of all doctoral research assistantships** are federally funded
- NIH 22,824 · NSF 19,229 · DoD 6,532 · DOE 5,320
- Only 8.8% are self-supporting

## The limit that must not be crossed

**The SED and GSS figures cannot be multiplied.** SED gives citizenship but not
whether a given assistantship is federal or institutional. GSS gives the federal
split but not citizenship. Combining them to produce a count of "federally
funded international students" assumes the federal share of assistantships is
identical for both groups, and nothing measures that.

If a reader asks how many international students federal grants pay for, the
answer is that this data cannot say, and the honest version is the one above:
the funded route runs mostly through assistantships, assistantships are heavily
federally funded in aggregate, and international students take that route far
more often than citizens do.

## What it does and does not establish

It establishes that the funded path through a US doctorate is used far more by
international students, and that self-funding is overwhelmingly domestic.

It does **not** establish intent, and it does not show that any individual
citizen was refused a funded place. Departments fund the students they admit;
this measures the result, not the decision. Nothing public records who applied
or who was admitted, so "prioritised" remains an inference from outcomes. Say
so if asked directly.

# After the degree: OPT, the H-1B cap, and permanent residence

What happens to graduates once they finish. Every figure below is federal
except where marked, and each carries the limit that constrains it.

## OPT — working on a student visa

DHS SEVP, calendar years. A complete national count, not a sample.

- OPT authorisations **24,838 (2007) → 194,554 (2024)**
- STEM OPT, the 24-month extension created in 2008: **2 → 95,384**
- total SEVIS records carrying OPT in 2024: **269,323**

Largest employers of OPT and STEM OPT students, 2024: **Amazon 10,167**
(5,379 OPT, 6,679 STEM OPT), University of California 2,916, Google 2,454,
Microsoft 2,143, Meta 2,032.

**The employer table is a Top 200 ranking, not a census.** An employer outside
it is absent rather than at zero, so it cannot be summed to a total. The
national series has no such problem.

## The payroll-tax differential

Under IRC 3121(b)(19), students in F-1 or J-1 status are exempt from Social
Security and Medicare on authorised employment, and IRS guidance lists
practical training — so OPT and STEM OPT are covered.

An employer hiring an OPT graduate therefore pays no employer-side FICA. The
same job filled by a U.S. worker carries **7.65%**, which is **$7,650 per
$100,000 of payroll**.

Say the rate, not a national total. No agency publishes OPT wages, so any
dollar figure is a scenario resting on an assumed wage. Two further limits:
the exemption ends when the holder becomes a resident alien, generally after
five years in F or J status, and those wages accrue no Social Security credits
for the worker.

## The cap, and who is exempt

The H-1B is limited to 85,000 new petitions a year. Three employer types are
exempt by statute (INA 214(g)(5)): institutions of higher education,
nonprofits related to or affiliated with one, and nonprofit or government
research organisations.

USCIS petitions filed, FY2018 → FY2025:

- higher education **19,484 → 26,949**
- **nonprofits affiliated with a university 10,734 → 24,073 (+124%)**
- nonprofit/government research organisations **5,595 → 7,560**

The affiliated-nonprofit route grew far faster than universities themselves,
and it does not require the university to employ anyone: the nonprofit's
affiliation is what confers the exemption.

**These come from an ACWIA FEE exemption table.** USCIS records cap status on
the I-129 and does not publish it. The employer rows are the same statutory
categories, but fee exemption is broader — the same table counts primary and
secondary schools, which are not cap-exempt — and the report's own footnote
warns a petition may appear under several headings, so the rows must never be
summed.

## Around the cap

Petitions approved, 2019 → 2023, the window where both agencies count the same
way:

- **H-1B −1%** (389,378 → 386,340)
- **O-1A +42%** (6,670 → 9,490) — extraordinary ability, uncapped

O-1 visas issued rose **6,712 (2005) → 19,457 (2024)**. L-1 intracompany
transfers fell **84,532 (2007) → 71,799 (2024)**, so the growth is specific to
the uncapped extraordinary-ability category rather than employment visas
generally.

EB-2 National Interest Waivers, which remove both the employer and the labour
certification: receipts **8,320 (FY2018) → 39,810 (FY2023)**, from 11.9% to
**42.8%** of all EB-2 filings.

## The route going the other way

J-1 research scholar needs no cap, no labour condition application and no wage
floor. It is shrinking: **36,083 (2015) → 24,067 (2024), −33%**. Professors
−47%, short-term scholars −31%, against −6% for J-1 overall. CY2025 is a
partial year and must not be used for a comparison.

## Permanent residence

PERM certifications, FY2024, 92,258 applications. The status the beneficiary
already held when the employer filed:

- **H-1B 57.6%**, not in the USA 10.0%, **F-1 7.9%**, L-1 4.4%

India 45.8% of applications, China 9.2%. Of the total, 50,814 certifications
lapsed unused against 35,505 live.

A certification is **not** a green card: USCIS must still approve an I-140 and
a visa number must be available.

## Do they stay?

NCSES matched the 2017–19 temporary-visa S&E doctorate cohort to
administrative records. Of 44,450, **32,650 were still in the United States in
2023 — 73%**. China 83%, India 86%, South Korea 50%, Turkey 53%.

This is measured residence. The site separately holds SED Table 2-8, which
records what recipients **said they intended** before graduating. The two are
different quantities and must not be quoted interchangeably.

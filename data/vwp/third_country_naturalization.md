# Third-Country Naturalization and the Visa Waiver Program

Research compiled: April 2026

This document examines how naturalization in VWP-member countries creates potential pathways
for nationals of non-VWP countries to gain visa-free entry to the United States.

---

## 1. Chile: Haitian Naturalization and VWP Exploitation

### Haitian Population in Chile

Chile experienced a massive influx of Haitian migrants following the 2010 Haiti earthquake:

| Year | Est. Haitian Population in Chile | Source |
|------|----------------------------------|--------|
| 2002 | 50 | Census |
| Pre-2013 | ~4,000 | Estimates |
| 2014-2016 | ~41,000 (731% increase) | Migration Policy Institute |
| 2017 | 105,000 | Government data |
| 2018 | ~120,000 | Government data |
| 2019 | 185,865 | Chile National Statistics Institute |
| 2020 | ~182,700 | Estimates |

Most Haitians entered Chile as tourists without visa requirements (visa-free entry was later
eliminated under President Pinera's anti-immigration platform). As of October 2021, approximately
70,000 Haitians held permanent residence in Chile, while another 110,000 lacked official
documentation.

### Naturalization Numbers

**Only 170 Haitians obtained Chilean citizenship between 2010 and October 2021.** This is an
extremely low naturalization rate relative to the ~185,000 Haitian population. Chile requires
5 years of continuous residency (or 2 years if married to a Chilean citizen) plus permanent
residency status, Spanish proficiency, clean criminal record, and financial stability. The full
process typically takes 7-8 years from arrival to citizenship.

### Children Born in Chile

A more significant pathway exists through children: Chile grants citizenship to anyone born on
its territory (jus soli). Haitian parents who arrived in 2014-2016 now have children aged 8-12
who are Chilean citizens by birth. These children will eventually have full access to Chilean
passports and VWP/ESTA travel. This is a longer-term concern (10+ years) but represents a
larger population than the 170 naturalized adults.

At least 41 children of Haitian descent born in Chile were documented as having been expelled
to Haiti during migration enforcement, creating diplomatic complications since they held
Chilean (not Haitian) citizenship.

### Onward Migration from Chile to the US

The primary onward migration pattern from Chile to the US has been **irregular**, not through
VWP/ESTA. In September 2021, thousands of Haitians who had been living in Chile for years
arrived at the US-Mexico border (Del Rio, TX crisis). These individuals traveled overland
through Central America and Mexico -- they did not use Chilean passports or ESTA.

**Assessment**: The Haitian-to-Chilean-citizen-to-VWP pipeline is currently **minimal** (170
naturalized citizens). The more significant risk is from Chilean-born children of Haitian parents
who will reach adulthood in the 2030s. There is no evidence that DHS has specifically flagged
Haitian-born Chilean citizens using ESTA as a concern; DHS concerns with Chile center on
burglary tourism by ethnic Chileans.

## 2. Chile VWP Status and Overstay Data

### Overstay Statistics (from DHS Entry/Exit Overstay Reports)

Chile has consistently had the **highest overstay rate of any VWP country**:

| Fiscal Year | Expected Departures | Total Overstays | Suspected In-Country | Total Rate | In-Country Rate |
|-------------|--------------------:|----------------:|---------------------:|-----------:|----------------:|
| FY2023 | 466,799 | 12,233 | 10,886 | 2.62% | 2.33% |
| FY2024 | 438,861 | 10,197 | 9,088 | 2.32% | 2.07% |

For comparison, FY2024 VWP averages and notable countries:

| Country | Expected Departures | Total Overstays | Total Rate | In-Country Rate |
|---------|--------------------:|----------------:|-----------:|----------------:|
| **Chile** | **438,861** | **10,197** | **2.32%** | **2.07%** |
| Portugal | 196,178 | 3,689 | 1.88% | 1.68% |
| Spain | 894,491 | 14,561 | 1.63% | 1.46% |
| Andorra | 1,427 | 16 | 1.12% | 1.12% |
| Greece | 90,136 | 879 | 0.98% | 0.75% |
| Israel | 373,198 | 2,967 | 0.80% | 0.70% |
| **VWP Average** | **18,853,231** | **93,079** | **0.49%** | **0.43%** |
| Japan | 1,517,793 | 1,509 | 0.10% | 0.08% |

Chile's 2.32% total overstay rate is **4.7x the VWP average** (0.49%).

### VWP Threshold: The 2% Rule

Countries with a 2% or greater overstay rate among business/tourism visitors must initiate a
public information campaign to reduce violations. At 2% or above, DHS has authority to restrict
or suspend VWP participation. Chile has exceeded this threshold in both FY2023 (2.62%) and
FY2024 (2.32%).

### Congressional Pressure for Chile's Removal

Only two countries have ever been removed from the VWP:
- **Argentina** (revoked 2002) -- overstay concerns
- **Uruguay** (revoked 2003) -- overstay concerns

Active congressional efforts to suspend Chile:

- **January 2025**: Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX) wrote to DHS Secretary Noem and Secretary of State
  Rubio urging reconsideration of Chile's VWP status
- **March 2025**: Bipartisan letter led by Rep. Young Kim (R-CA) with Reps. Correa and Levin
  (D-CA) demanding suspension until Chile fully complies with the 2013 PCSC Information
  Sharing Agreement
- **Michigan**: State legislature passed HR 269 calling for Chile's removal

### "Burglary Tourism"

South American Theft Groups (SATGs), primarily Chilean nationals, exploit VWP/ESTA to enter
the US, commit residential and commercial burglaries, and return to Chile. Key data points:

- Orange County DA charged 14 Chileans with burglaries after VWP entry
- In 2024, Orange County Sheriff reported 59 residential burglaries linked to SATGs
- Chile has not fully complied with the PCSC agreement to share criminal background data
- ESTA costs $21 vs. $185 for a standard visa, reducing the vetting barrier

### Current Status (April 2026)

Chile remains in the VWP. DHS Secretary Noem met with Chilean officials and praised Chile's
compliance efforts. Chile passed a motion urging domestic public awareness of ESTA compliance.
No formal suspension has been announced.

## 3. Canada: Naturalization and Visa-Free US Entry

Canada naturalizes approximately 350,000-380,000 new citizens per year:

| 2024 | 375,099 |

| 2 | Philippines | 14,309 |

| Country | Population | Share |
|---------|----------:|------:|
| India | 898,045 | 10.7% |
| Philippines | 719,580 | 8.6% |
| China | 715,835 | 8.6% |

### Canada-US Entry Dynamics

Canada is **not** a VWP country but has a separate, more permissive arrangement:
- Canadian citizens can enter the US visa-free for business or tourism (B-1/B-2 equivalent)
- No ESTA requirement
- Entry at land borders involves minimal documentation (passport or NEXUS card)
- The vast majority of Canada-US crossings are by land, where exit tracking is limited

**DHS FY2024 overstay data for Canada:**
- Expected departures (air/sea only): 9,429,208
- Suspected in-country overstay rate: 0.19%
- Note: This captures only air/sea travelers; the much larger land crossing population is
  tracked through a data-sharing agreement with CBSA, with CBP matching 98.17% of
  Canadian entry records

**Key gap**: DHS overstay reports do not track country-of-birth for Canadian citizens entering
the US. A Canadian citizen born in China, India, or Iran enters on their Canadian passport and
is counted as "Canada" in all statistics. There is no publicly available data on how many
naturalized Canadian citizens (by birth country) enter the US visa-free.

### Assessment

Canada naturalizes ~375,000 people annually, with India, Philippines, China, Nigeria, and Iran
as top source countries. All of these naturalized citizens gain visa-free access to the US. This
is not generally treated as a security concern because:

1. Canada's naturalization process includes security screening (CSIS checks)
2. The US-Canada intelligence sharing relationship is extensive (Five Eyes)
3. Canada's overall overstay rate (0.19%) is among the lowest
4. US CBP can still deny entry to any individual regardless of citizenship

However, the scale is significant: roughly 40,000+ Chinese-born and 35,000+ Indian-born
individuals become Canadian citizens each year, gaining visa-free US access in the process.

## 4. Third-Country Naturalization as a VWP Loophole

### The Mechanism

A national of a non-VWP country (e.g., China, Russia, Iran) who obtains citizenship in a VWP
country gains access to ESTA/VWP travel to the US. This is sometimes called "passport shopping"
or "citizenship arbitrage."

### ESTA Screening Mitigations

The ESTA application collects:
- Country of citizenship
- Other citizenships (dual/multiple)
- How citizenship was acquired (birth vs. naturalization)
- Country of birth

ESTA continuously vets applicants against the Terrorist Screening Database, lost/stolen passport
records, visa revocations, and previous VWP refusals. **VWP nationals who also hold citizenship
from Cuba, DPRK, Iran, Iraq, Sudan, or Syria are automatically ineligible for ESTA** under the
Visa Waiver Program Improvement and Terrorist Travel Prevention Act of 2015.

### Hungary: The Precedent Case

Hungary's simplified naturalization program (enacted 2011) is the clearest example of DHS
responding to third-country naturalization concerns:

- **Scale**: Nearly 1 million people naturalized 2011-2020 through a simplified process
- **Source populations**: Ethnic Hungarians in Serbia, Ukraine, Romania, Slovakia -- approximately
  5 million eligible
- **DHS concern**: Inadequate identity verification in the simplified process
- **US response (August 2023)**: DHS reduced ESTA validity for Hungarian passport holders from
  2 years to 1 year, limited to a single entry per ESTA (normally multi-entry)
- **Rationale**: "Despite extensive efforts over many years by the U.S. government to resolve
  longstanding security issues arising from Hungary's simplified naturalization process, the
  Hungarian government has opted not to address the concerns."

This is the **only instance** where DHS has specifically penalized a VWP country for its
naturalization policies.

### Portugal: Golden Visa Program

Portugal's Golden Visa program (launched October 2012) allowed foreign investors to obtain
residency and eventually citizenship:

- **Total main applicants (2012-2023)**: 12,718 (plus 20,424 dependents)
- **Total investment**: ~EUR 7.3 billion
- **Top nationalities**:
  - China: 5,319 visas (dominant by far)
  - Brazil: 1,203
  - United States: 635
  - Turkey: 571
  - South Africa: 532
- **Citizenship conversions**: Over 2,000 Golden Visa holders obtained Portuguese citizenship
- **Portugal VWP overstay rate (FY2024)**: 1.88% -- second highest among VWP countries

Portugal's program is notable because Chinese nationals represent **42% of all Golden Visa
recipients**. After 5 years of residency, these individuals can obtain Portuguese citizenship and
gain VWP/ESTA access to the US. Portugal approved 98.5% of citizenship applications in 2022.

The real estate investment pathway was ended in 2023, but the fund investment pathway continues.
The share of investment funds rose from 0.6% (2019) to 28.6% (2023).

### Malta: Citizenship by Investment (Terminated)

Malta ran a Citizenship by Investment (CBI) program that was terminated after an ECJ ruling
in April 2025:

- **Total citizenships granted (2013-2024)**: Over 28,000
- **Nationality breakdown**: 56% Russian/former Soviet Union, 19% Middle Eastern, 13% other
  Asian, 7% African
- **Peak years**: 3,610 (2018), 3,186 (2017)
- **Current status**: Program ruled illegal by ECJ; replaced with "Citizenship by Merit" rules
  in July 2025. New applicants cannot obtain citizenship solely by financial investment.

Malta's program was explicitly flagged by the EU Commission as creating risks of "corruption,
money laundering, security threats and tax avoidance." The Russia-Ukraine conflict in 2022
highlighted that Russian oligarchs could use Maltese passports to evade sanctions.

### Other VWP Countries with Notable Naturalization Pathways

| Country | Residency Requirement | Dual Citizenship | Notes |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------|-------|
| Portugal | 5 years | Yes | Golden Visa pathway; 1.88% overstay rate |
| Ireland | 5 years | Yes | No language test required |
| Germany | 5 years (3 if married) | Yes (since 2024) | New dual citizenship law |
| Spain | 10 years (2 for Latin Americans) | Limited | Fast track for Ibero-American nationals |
| Italy | 10 years (4 for EU) | Yes | Ancestry-based citizenship common |
| Hungary | Simplified (no residency) | Yes | DHS already restricted ESTA |
| Chile | 5 years | Yes | Highest VWP overstay rate |

## 5. Key Findings and Risk Assessment

### Current Risks (Active)

1. **Chile VWP overstay rate**: At 2.32% (FY2024), Chile exceeds the 2% threshold. Roughly
   9,000 Chileans per year are suspected in-country overstays. Congressional pressure for
   removal is bipartisan. Chile's refusal to fully share criminal data via the PCSC agreement
   compounds the concern.

2. **Portugal Golden Visa to citizenship pipeline**: Over 5,300 Chinese nationals received
   Golden Visas; 2,000+ have obtained Portuguese citizenship. These individuals now have
   VWP/ESTA access to the US. Portugal has the second-highest VWP overstay rate (1.88%).

3. **Hungary simplified naturalization**: ~1 million people naturalized with minimal vetting.
   DHS has responded by restricting Hungarian ESTA validity. This affects ethnic Hungarians
   from Ukraine and Serbia who may have limited identity documentation.

### Emerging Risks

4. **Chilean-born children of Haitian parents**: Tens of thousands of children born to Haitian
   immigrants in Chile since 2014 are Chilean citizens by birth. They will reach adulthood in
   the 2030s and gain VWP/ESTA access.

5. **Canada's high-volume naturalization**: ~375,000 new citizens per year, with India, China,
   and Iran as top source countries. All gain visa-free US access. While Canada's overstay
   rate is low (0.19%), the sheer volume creates a large population with visa-free US access
   whose origins are not tracked in public DHS data.

### Data Gaps

- DHS does not publicly report VWP/ESTA usage by country of birth, only by country of
  citizenship. This makes it impossible to assess how many ESTA travelers were born in
  non-VWP countries.
- Canada-US land border crossings are not included in overstay reports.
- Chile's naturalization data (by country of origin of naturalizing citizens) is not
  systematically published.
- No public DHS reports specifically address third-country naturalization as a VWP risk
  factor, except for the Hungary case.

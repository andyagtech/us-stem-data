Research & Innovation

  • Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering
  • Research & Innovation

The Allen School advances research excellence from the foundations of computing to emerging applications that are shaping the future of not just our field but also our broader society.

We have deep technical expertise that touches every aspect of computing, and we have a long and proud tradition of interdisciplinary collaboration on cross-cutting research with real-world impact. Our researchers continue to build on this tradition while opening up new avenues of exploration, harnessing the transformative power of computing to develop solutions that benefit people, society and the planet while expanding the frontiers of our field.

Q&A: Security and privacy impacts of technology for incarcerated people

Allen School Ph.D. student Yael Eiger and professor Franziska Roesner discuss their research examining people’s experiences with technology during and after incarceration and the broader impacts of technology used for policing and post-release monitoring.

Allen School News

Professor Franziska Roesner receives SOUPS Impact Award

In their 2017 paper, Roesner and her co-authors were among the first to investigate end-user security and privacy perspectives on then-emerging smart home technologies and recommend ways to help users make more informed security decisions.



Read the story »

Allen School News

Five faculty members receive NSF CAREER Awards

Professors Andrea Coladangelo, Matthew Golub, Ranjay Krishna, Chinmay Nirkhe, and Stephanie Wang are shaping the future of computing through projects spanning AI, systems, computational neuroscience, cryptography and quantum computing.



Read the story »

Shayan Oveis Gharan: Winner, 2026 IMU Abacus Medal

Oveis Gharan, a member of the Allen School’s Theory of Computation Group, received the Abacus Medal from the International Mathematical Union for his landmark contributions to the theory of algorithms. The Abacus Medal is awarded every four years to a mathematician under the age of 40 who has made significant advancements in mathematical aspects of information sciences.
